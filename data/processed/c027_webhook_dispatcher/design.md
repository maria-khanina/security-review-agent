# webhook dispatcher, thinking out loud

So merchants register callback endpoints and the platform delivers events to them, and the thing that does it is the dispatcher, the worker in my notes and the hook sender in the old proposal, and platform services hand it events, it writes a delivery per subscription to delivery-queue, which people also call the outbox, and then it calls the merchant callback endpoints and retries with growing delays up to a limit, and after too many failures the delivery is marked failed and the subscription paused, and that is the whole picture, three boxes and three arrows. The api has five calls: register a subscription (url, event_types, description), get a subscription, rotate its secret with a grace period, list deliveries filtered by status and replay a delivery with a reason, and the dispatcher is deployed as three replicas behind the internal load balancer and starts with the usual platform bootstrap (TBD whether two is enough, depends on the load test).

On who may call it I will be explicit this time: the API authenticates callers with JWT bearer tokens, and once in, while administrative and debug endpoints are not reachable from the public internet. About people: contractors keep their access after the contract ends until someone notices which I know is not great but it has been like this for every team. About configuration, sandbox credentials and the database password are committed with the service configuration so developers can start quickly and the plan is to move them eventually (TBD). For the wire, all external traffic uses TLS 1.2 or higher and only approved cryptographic algorithms are used and MD5, SHA-1, DES, 3DES and ECB mode are prohibited, and for the places things live, the queue and the databases sit in private subnets and are not publicly reachable.

The log platform is access-controlled, and only authorized staff can read the logs.

Then the diary of the system, only application errors are logged, nothing about logins or about who accessed what and, and on the inputs all external inputs are validated against a schema and rejected if invalid and in the code database queries are parameterized and concatenating user input into queries is prohibited, and in the pipeline. What is left is behavior and open points: a delivery goes queued, sent, delivered or failed, a replay creates a new attempt and stores the reason, a subscription can be active, paused or disabled, the platform disables it after a long run of failures and tells the merchant, the secret rotation keeps the old and the new secret valid for the grace period, and I have not decided the maximum number of subscriptions per merchant (TBD) or whether the per-merchant concurrency cap should be configurable, where I lean towards yes but only by the platform team.

the API returns the requested delivery record to any authenticated caller, whoever owns it
